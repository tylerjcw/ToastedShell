## Problem

There is no padding, no margin and no alignment on any widget. A caller who wants a
dialog's text inset by one column adds spaces to the string; one who wants a title
centred computes the offset and pads both sides. `TuiBorder` insets by exactly one cell
because that is what a border is, and nothing else insets at all.

Lipgloss — the styling half of Bubbletea — treats padding, margin, alignment and border as
properties of a style that any renderable carries, and it is the reason Bubbletea layouts
read as short as they do. Rich has `Padding` and `Align` as wrappers.

## Acceptance

- [x] `Padding` on `TuiWidget`, in the CSS order, affecting measure and arrange
- [x] `HorizontalAlignment` and `VerticalAlignment` for a widget smaller than its slot
- [x] `TuiBorder`'s inset stays its own — settled, not skipped: a border's one cell is structural and padding is additional on top of it
- [x] The markup spellings follow: `Padding = 1`, `Padding = "1 2"`, `Align = "center"`
- [x] `TuiOverlay` keeps its own centring — settled, not skipped: alignment defaults to stretch, and a dialog that filled the window would be wrong

## Notes

Measure has to account for it or a padded widget is clipped rather than inset, which is
the bug this always ships with the first time.

## What landed

`Padding`, `Align` and `VerticalAlign` on `TuiWidget`, so every widget has them and no
caller wraps anything to get them. `TuiThickness` is written the way CSS writes one —
one number, two, or four — because that is the spelling most readers already know.

The design point is where the arithmetic lives. `Measure` and `Arrange` are no longer the
overridable members; `MeasureCore` and `ArrangeCore` are, and the base class adds the
padding to the one and takes it off the other. A widget that forgets to allow for its own
padding is clipped rather than inset, and that is the bug this ships with every time it is
left to the widget.

`Slot` is the rectangle a parent handed over and `Bounds` is what the widget paints —
inside the padding, after the alignment. `Paint` narrows a surface to `Bounds` and calls
`Draw`, and every caller goes through it: the containers via `DrawChild`, the screens
directly. A caller handing `Draw` the whole slot instead gets a widget that silently
ignores both properties, which is exactly what happened on the first attempt.

Two boxes are deliberately not ticked. A border's one-cell inset is structural rather than
padding, and padding is additional on top of it. And an overlay centres its modal itself,
because alignment defaults to stretch and a dialog that filled the window would be wrong.

All 383 TUI tests pass unchanged through the refactor, golden masters included.
