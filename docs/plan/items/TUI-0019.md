## Problem

`TuiLength` offers `Fixed`, `Auto` and `Star`. The help browser wants "a third of the
width, but never less than 24 and never more than 40", and writes:

```csharp
var listWidth = Math.Clamp(width / 3, 24, 40);
```

then assigns it as a fixed length, which means the pane stops being a third of anything
the moment the terminal is resized past either bound. Every caller with an opinion about
proportions has to recompute it per frame and hand back a number.

Ratatui's constraints are `Length`, `Percentage`, `Ratio`, `Min`, `Max` and `Fill`, and
its solver satisfies them together. That is the same job `TuiStack.Distribute` already
does for fixed and star; it is the vocabulary that is short.

## Acceptance

- [x] `TuiLength.Percent(n)` — a share of the container, not of what is left over
- [x] `Min` and `Max` bounds, composable with the other kinds
- [x] The string spellings follow: `"33%"`, `"2*"`, `"12"`, `"auto"`, and a bound like `"1/3 24..40"`
- [x] `TuiStack.Distribute` satisfies bounds before shares, and says what it did when it cannot
- [x] The help browser's `Math.Clamp` disappears

## Notes

Worth doing before many scripts are written against sizes, for the reason `TUI-0016` was:
the spelling is the part that is hard to change afterwards.

## What landed

`Fraction` as a kind, reached two ways: `Percent(33)` when the number is round, and
`Ratio(1, 3)` when it is not. A third is not 33% — 118 columns by 33/100 is 38 and by 1/3
is 39 — and the author who wrote `width / 3` meant the second. Ratatui carries both for
the same reason.

`Minimum` and `Maximum` are on the length rather than at the call site, so they compose
with every kind: a fixed length that must not exceed the room, an auto one that must not
collapse, a star share that must stay readable. The spelling is a range after a space —
`"1/3 24..40"`, `"* ..40"`, `"auto 10.."` — which is how a range reads elsewhere in the
language.

The solver settles everything with an answer of its own first, in the order written and
each clamped, and only then shares the remainder. The rounding error goes to a star child
that can absorb it: one pinned by a bound cannot, and handing it the remainder anyway is
how a bounded pane ends up one column wider than it asked for.

The help browser's `Math.Clamp(width / 3, 24, 40)` is now `Size = "1/3 24..40"`, said once
where the pane is built rather than recomputed per frame. Its sidebar is one column
narrower than before, because a fraction is measured against the space actually being
divided — the gap between the panes comes out first. No text changed anywhere in the four
recorded sessions; only box-drawing characters moved.
